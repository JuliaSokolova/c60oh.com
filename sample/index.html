01 / The scientific foundation

Fullerenes: a different form of carbon.

Fullerenes are cage-shaped carbon molecules, including C₆₀. Their discovery was recognized by the 1996 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Hydroxylated fullerenes, often called fullerenols, incorporate hydroxyl groups that change their interactions with water.

Some fullerene derivatives have shown redox and skin-cell effects in experimental studies. These findings depend on the exact derivative and formulation; they cannot be assumed for C60(OH) hydrogel.

06 / Industry challenge

The EU regulatory challenge for fullerene-based cosmetics.

Fullerene-based materials offer intriguing possibilities for cosmetic science, but commercialization in Europe requires rigorous material-specific safety evidence and clarity about nanomaterial classification.

What the EU scientific review says

The European Commission’s Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety (SCCS) reviewed fullerenes, hydroxylated fullerenes and hydrated forms of hydroxylated fullerenes in nanoform. Its 2023 opinion identified significant data limitations and concerns including genotoxicity; it did not establish these materials as safe for cosmetic use under the assessed conditions.

Results from one fullerene preparation cannot automatically establish the safety of another. Particle identity, size distribution, aggregation, surface chemistry, impurities and exposure all matter.

Read the European Commission SCCS opinion ↗

How C60(OH) Labs is addressing it

  • Material identity: validate composition, fullerene-derived content and batch specifications.
  • Nanoform assessment: establish particle-size distributions, aggregation state and relevant physicochemical properties.
  • Biological safety: complete appropriate cytotoxicity and further toxicological studies.
  • Formulation safety: assess stability, exposure, microbiology and intended-use conditions.
  • Regulatory pathway: determine applicable EU cosmetic nanomaterial obligations before any market use.

C60(OH) is currently supplied for laboratory research evaluation. Its regulatory classification, safety and suitability for cosmetic use have not been established. This section describes an industry-wide challenge, not an approved safety finding for C60(OH).

08 / Sample evaluation

What should your team test next?

  1. Review available technical and handling documentation.
  2. Evaluate pH, appearance, dilution and compatibility in your formulation system.
  3. Measure stability, rheology, and ingredient interactions.
  4. Run validated cytotoxicity and ROS/redox assays before biological claims.
  5. Compare against matched controls, including commercial fullerene ingredients if relevant.
